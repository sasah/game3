# Rendering techniques and "cheats" for 2.5D games on pre-rendered / painted backgrounds (state as of Oct 2026)

Scope note for the report writer: the project is a phone-browser three.js/WebGL2 game. Each location is one isometric painting (orthographic, 45° yaw / 30° pitch) generated by an image model over a 3D blockout. The game already has: blockout depth written to the depth buffer, Depth-Anything depth calibrated to the blockout, derived normals, material masks (water, foliage, windows), UV-distortion water, leaf sway, window flicker, ground mist, cloud shadows, a candle point light, camera zoom to points of interest with a sharper repainted inset, and parallax relief. Findings below are grouped by the five key questions. "Historical" means shipped 1998–2016. "Current" means 2020–2026.

## 1. How shipped games built painted or pre-rendered backgrounds and integrated characters (passes, relighting, day/night, walk-behind, shadows, water/fog, color matching, reflections)

### Takeaway
Every shipped "painted background + live characters" game has a hidden proxy of the scene. That proxy is a set of pre-rendered passes: depth for per-pixel occlusion, normals for relighting, albedo plus a separate shadow/light layer for day/night, and low-res control maps for character lighting and shadow. Integration is mostly faked with cheap tricks: scene-matched key and fill lights, ambient color sampled from the background, blob or decal shadows, and tinted shadows. Animated parts (water, plants, critters) live on separate planes or layers, not in the painting. The project already follows the Pillars of Eternity / Disco Elysium template. The parts most worth copying are Disco Elysium's separate shadow layer for day/night and Pillars' low-res "sun contribution" map for characters.

### Cited Findings

**Pillars of Eternity 1 (2015) and Tyranny (2016), Obsidian, Unity. Historical but the closest template**
- Backgrounds are rendered offline in Maya as 2D images in four passes: final, depth, normal and albedo. Unity combines them "for per-pixel occlusion of 3D objects, and for real-time dynamic lighting." The raw data is "sometimes over several gigabytes", so a compression step runs before import. — [Obsidian Update #79: Graphics and Rendering](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)
- The 2D look only holds from one angle: "The illusion comes together only when an orthographic camera is placed at the perfect angle." In the Unity editor the world looks skewed. — [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)
- Character lighting uses two directional lights tuned per scene to match the pre-rendered lighting. The key light matches the sun and is modified by the day/night cycle to cast moonlight. A fill light keeps the character's back from going black. Dynamic deferred lights (torches, fireball bursts) light both the 2D background and 3D characters. — [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)
- Ambient (cheap GI): "a system samples color from the 2D background" as a fast approximation of GI for characters. — [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)
- Shadow control: "The new system samples a low resolution image map which controls the contribution of the directional sunlight," and "the same image controls the value of the dynamic shadow map." This stops characters standing in painted shade from being sunlit, and avoids double shadows. Dynamic shadows are tinted to the painted shadow color, often bluish. Obsidian added this because "3D characters were not matching the 2D rendered scene as much as they had hoped." An optional subtle bloom applies to both environment and characters. — [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)
- Lead designer J.E. Sawyer told modders they would need to render their own 3D sources, because "you need the depth and normal information for occlusion and dynamic lighting." — [Obsidian forum thread](https://forums.obsidian.net/topic/65116-i-have-a-question-about-the-backgrounds-for-the-game-2d3d/)
- A former Obsidian artist says everything was pre-rendered in Maya/Mental Ray except a few animated elements, and that render targets (normal, depth, albedo, spec) were output as textures so the scene could be relit in real time. — [Polycount "Pillars of Eternity mini-dump"](https://polycount.com/discussion/150819/pillars-of-eternity-mini-dump) (page returned 403 to me; content from search snippet)
- Scale and art rules: backgrounds were about "10,000 by 10,000 pixels" and took days to render on a "fat renderer farm". Geometry had "millions and millions of really high poly, highly dense geometry." An engineer wrote a pixel shader replicating the Infinity Engine look. Front-facing interior walls were removed and wall tops painted black, so rooms float in a "black void". Ramps had to rise toward the top of the image, because ramps sloping away lost depth. The look was tuned on one tavern interior and that rule set reused for dungeons. — [GameBanshee: Obsidian on modernizing isometric art](http://www.gamebanshee.com/kdh63)
- Tyranny reused the same pre-rendered isometric backdrop approach. I found no Tyranny-specific technical write-up. — [CyberPowerPC review](https://www.cyberpowerpc.com/blog/tyranny-review-an-outstanding-fantasy-extravaganza-of-a-darker-shade/); [Unity forum, user speculation](https://discussions.unity.com/t/how-did-the-obsidian-developers-do-the-art-for-tyranny-and-pillars-of-eternity/697110)

**Pillars of Eternity II: Deadfire (2018)**
- New features: dynamic weather in which "trees, ocean, VFX particles and capes all dynamically react to wind"; "Clouds will cast shadows on the level as they roll by"; parallaxing background layers ("we can now place VFX and blur them together with the backgrounds"); shader parity so torches produce "tight highlights [that] bloom on metals and dance over patches of wet med", which they called "not 100%, but … pretty darn close"; antialiasing and AO that "really help the characters blend"; and new translucent/refractive shaders for water and ice on 3D assets. — [Fig Update #11 via RPG Codex](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=10542)
- Josh Sawyer named the fully dynamic weather system one of the two biggest features. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/pillars-of-eternity-2-deadfire-sets-sail-for-frontiers-beyond-mere-tradition/)
- Counterpoint: one review saw "no drastic change in graphics" in environments versus PoE1. Most of the visual leap was in spell and weapon VFX. — [Shacknews review](https://shacknews.com/article/104815/pillars-of-eternity-2-deadfire-review-sailing-into-superiority)

**Disco Elysium (2019; Final Cut 2021), ZA/UM, Unity**
- They used "very basic 3D geometry as a canvas" under the 2D painting, so they would not have to paint separate day/night/snow/rain images. The canvas also gives partly real-time shadows and placement feedback for characters. — [ZA/UM devblog "Weather Duty", 2016](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2016/02/10/weather-duty)
- Day/night is handled with a split light layer. Shadows are baked for several times of day and stored in RGB channels: "Lack of a color means that spot is in shadow and the brightness of red, green and blue means the area is lit at the corresponding time of day at that intensity." The channels are multiplied onto the background albedo. Interiors have a fixed light direction (from the windows), and time of day only changes intensity and warmth through a "lightflow map", so one shadow map serves the whole day. Exteriors need different shaders because the sun moves. State switches are hidden with time skips, overcast and light/shadow color grading. — [Weather Duty](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2016/02/10/weather-duty)
- Weather is generated procedurally from sine functions (temperature, dew point, precipitation = dew point minus temperature). Rules: rain if precipitation > 0 and temperature > 0; snow if precipitation > 0 and temperature < 0; lightning if precipitation > 1.5 and temperature > 2. A top-down shadow map masks rain so covered areas stay dry, and wind is faked by tilting that caster. Wetness goes into the spec map. Edge detection places splashes under eaves and object edges. Characters cast real-time shadows onto the background via the simple geometry (still "needs work" in 2016). — [Weather Duty](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2016/02/10/weather-duty)
- Animated, pickable or time-specific things are added in real time, not painted. Examples: NPCs, vegetation, collectables, particles, fish swarms, ripples, wobbling icebergs, seagulls. The sea is a flat plane with a special shader for coast edge fading. A recolorable warm gradient overlay suggests shallow, murky water and carries a flow map for current. Plants are hand-painted on transparent planes ("Since the camera angle is always the same"), and "every plant that's in the water is basically a transparent plane with some distortion effect." — [From Render to Paintover, via GameBanshee](https://www.gamebanshee.com/news/121823-disco-elysium-from-render-to-paintover.html) (original: zaumstudio.com/2019/01/23/from-render-to-paintover/)
- Scale: about 193 squares of 4K environment textures. Quadruple that to include each painting's height, normal and shadow maps. The character pipeline was rebuilt 3–4 times. — [MCV/Develop interview with Kaspar Tamsalu](https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/we-knew-immediately-that-we-needed-to-make-a-game-with-a-striking-and-unique-look-to-accompany-the-writing-a-look-that-would-balance-the-mundane-with-the-unfamiliar-and-strange-the-art)

**Baldur's Gate 1/2, Planescape: Torment (Infinity Engine, 1998–2000) and Enhanced Editions (2012+). Historical**
- The engine shows huge pre-rendered, scrolling 2D backgrounds with sprite characters. — [Wikipedia: Infinity Engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinity_Engine)
- The official feature list names a "clipping map for foreground effects", animated background tiles, search maps, 3D height maps, luminosity maps, dynamic lighting and real-time light sourcing. — [Planet Baldur's Gate features (archive)](https://pbg.bgforge.net/bg/info/features/index.html)
- Walk-behind has no depth buffer. Each area's WED file stores wall polygons "to determine when to dither creature animations (to represent moving 'behind' a wall)". Polygon flags cover shade wall, hovering, cover animations and door, and "the default behaviour is that wall shades animations from both sides." Doors have separate open and closed polygon sets. The map is a grid of 64×64 tile cells. Overlays (rivers, lakes) are separate animated tile grids stacked on the base grid. — [IESDP WED v1.3](https://gibberlings3.github.io/iesdp/file_formats/ie_formats/wed_v1.3.htm)
- Each area has companion bitmaps: `…LM.BMP` light map, `…SR.BMP` search map (walkability and material) and `…HT.BMP` height map. The ARE header has "Day/night" and "Extended night" flags, plus rain, snow, fog and lightning probabilities. Fog is BGEE only. — [IESDP ARE v1](https://gibberlings3.github.io/iesdp/file_formats/ie_formats/are_v1.htm)
- Night versions of areas have their own WED/TIS and light, search and height maps. The Enhanced Editions store tilesets as PVRZ (GPU-compressed) textures. — [SHS "mirror game maps" tool notes](https://www.shsforums.net/files/file/1163-mirror-game-maps)
- In EE, background brightness, contrast and gamma are separate from "dynamic stuff such as creatures, visual effects or animations", so creatures are lit separately from the painted map. — [Beamdog forum, Lighting Pack mod](https://forums.beamdog.com/discussion/comment/1047841)

**INSIDE (2016) and Limbo (2010), Playdead, Unity. Best primary source on cheap atmosphere**
- Renderer: "vanilla light-prepass rendering (unity: 'Legacy deferred')" with a single grab pass for translucency. "Fog turned out to be very central to our artstyle… Many of the initial scenes in the game were literally just fog + silhouettes." It is "Simple linear fog!", clamped to a maximum so bright objects (headlights, spotlights) "bleed through". — [Gjøel & Svendsen, "Low Complexity, High Fidelity – INSIDE Rendering" slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf); [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1023002/Low-Complexity-High-Fidelity-INSIDE)
- Glare/bloom: an old-school, low-threshold "buttery lens" LDR glow creates the mist. Two separate glow passes are used, one wide fog glow and one narrow glow, because a single weighted pass made them interfere. Bloom downsampling uses 9 instead of 13 taps by exploiting bilinear filtering (saved about 0.1 ms). TAA feeds the bloom so the bloom mask is antialiased. Chromatic or radial offsets take 3 samples at 1080p. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- Local volumetric light: box fog volumes that add only scattered light, never extra fog. Naive raymarching needed about 128 samples (3.2 ms). With blue-noise jitter and TAA, 3 samples replace 32. The pass runs in a half-resolution buffer that the translucency pass reuses. Animated caustics come from an animated light cookie. "This technique is primarily bandwidth bound." — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- Grounding characters without shadow maps:
  - Bounce lights: half-Lambert "wrap" point lights fake GI.
  - Multiplicative AO decals replace SSAO, for local control.
  - Shadow decals are box decals with a projected soft alpha. Anchored to the boy's feet they give "a very OOT blob shadow look", avoiding point-light shadow cost. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- Reflections: SSR decals with Fresnel opacity, plus "stitching" to fill occluded gaps behind the boy. Water uses planar (oblique-clip) reflections because SSR has too many off-screen misses on large surfaces. Water is composited in layers: murk fog, then refraction (one stochastic blue-noise sample along the normal), then reflection with Fresnel. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- Particles: smoke is lit only by a vertical gradient (top lighter) plus a GI point light. Motion comes from a "swirl noise" texture (Photoshop twirl on a UV gradient) projected in world space and scrolled. Fire is drawn into the alpha/bloom channel only, then mapped through a gradient LUT so color always matches luminance ("no dark whites… no bright blues or reds"). Flame flipbooks are 3×3: column chosen sequentially, row randomly, cross-faded with a noisy gradient phase. Lens-flare sprites are occluded by sampling the depth buffer in the vertex shader (32 samples on a golden-ratio spiral, only 4 vertices). Rain and dust are pure vertex-shader animation: each drop's position is seeded by the integer part of time and animated over the fractional part. This avoids CPU particles and billboard overdraw. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- Banding: "almost always dither any 8bit write". They dither after every pass with triangular-PDF (TPDF) or blue noise, including 8-bit G-buffer normals. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf); [Bart Wronski on TPDF dithering](https://bartwronski.com/2016/10/30/dithering-part-three-real-world-2d-quantization-dithering/)
- TAA: a talk on reprojection with disocclusion and trailing fixes. The code was open-sourced. — [GDC Vault: TRAA in INSIDE](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1022970/Temporal-Reprojection-Anti-Aliasing-in)
- Limbo: monochrome, heavy depth-of-field shaders and a film-grain shader with clear foreground/midground/background layers. Physics is Box2D. — [Univ. Bayreuth slides on Limbo](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/assets/Lehre/Wintersemester-2012-13/klsg6/KLSG6-Limbo.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbo_(video_game))

**Pentiment (2022), Obsidian**
- Pitched as 2D in the style of European block prints. Hand-drawn final art was too slow to revise, so iteration used grey uncolored roughs. Movement is restricted to tracks, which controls framing and depth. Paths leading "up" into the scene were avoided, and characters were not scaled to fake distance. Scenes almost always face a fixed "north" orientation so area transitions stay readable. There are 157 rigged characters with hand-drawn facial expressions. — [Game Developer: Deep Dive, the art of Pentiment (Hannah Kennedy)](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-the-art-of-pentiment)
- The book/page-turn presentation uses 3D meshes with bones: "everything that's animated is essentially a bone… these are all 3D objects with very complex geometric meshes." — [GameBanshee: Josh Sawyer interview](https://gamebanshee.com/9ak6r)

**Kentucky Route Zero (2013–2020), Cardboard Computer, Unity**

### Inferences
- The project's existing setup (blockout depth into the z-buffer, estimated normals, masks) is essentially Pillars' depth/normal/albedo set. The cheapest high-impact additions, in priority order:
  1. A low-res sun/shade control map (PoE). For example a 256–512 px grayscale texture in ground-plane space, sampled at the character's feet, that scales the character's key light and its shadow. It can be computed from the painting's luminance or from a shadow render of the blockout.
  2. Ambient color sampled from the painting around the character, via a blurred mip lookup at screen position (PoE). It gives automatic color matching under Kuindzhi-style moonlight.
  3. Characters' shadows tinted to the painting's shadow hue, not black (PoE).
  4. INSIDE-style blob or decal shadows under feet instead of shadow maps.
- For day/night or "lamp lit / unlit" variants, the Disco Elysium RGB-channel shadow trick fits the project well. One RGBA texture can hold up to four baked lighting states (for example dusk, night, night with windows lit, lightning flash) multiplied over a single "unlit" albedo. The image model can generate "same scene, lit differently" variants, which then become the masks. This is an inference; the DE team painted theirs.
- For a non-artist pipeline, Pentiment's constraints (fixed "north", movement on tracks, no paths going "up" into the image, no fake scaling) and Pillars' rule that ramps must rise toward the top of the image are cheap readability rules that also hide depth-estimation errors.
- Walk-behind: the z-buffer method beats IE's polygons, but IE-style hand-authored walk-behind polygons are a good fallback where estimated depth is wrong (thin posts, fences, eaves). For example, a per-location list of "cover polygons" with a fixed depth.

### Gaps
- Divinity: Original Sin and Wasteland 3 were not researched. Both are commonly described as fully real-time 3D (Larian's Divinity Engine; Unity for Wasteland 3), so they are less relevant to painted plates. Not verified here.
- No primary technical source found for Darkest Dungeon's lighting, Hades' environment pipeline, Kentucky Route Zero's shaders (only second-hand), Norco's process, Pentiment's character rig tools or engine details, or Owlcat's Kingmaker pipeline.
- Pillars' depth encoding format and background tile sizes are not published in Update #79.
- I found no shipped-game source on reflections of characters in painted water. INSIDE's planar and SSR approaches are for real 3D water.

## 2. Camera language in 2.5D (zoom, parallax layers, "breathing", projection mapping, single-image 3D moves, transitions, insets, viewpoint changes)

### Takeaway
Shipped painted-background games keep the camera nearly fixed: one orthographic angle, with scrolling and occasional zoom. Depth comes from parallax layers (Banner Saga, Deadfire) or from moving on authored tracks (Pentiment). Larger moves on a single painting use film camera projection: project the painting onto rough geometry and move the camera only a little. The research line from 3D Ken Burns to one-shot 3D Photography and LDI inpainting shows how far you can go before holes appear: small moves are free, large ones need inpainted layers. As of 2025–2026, single-image-to-Gaussian-splat world models (World Labs Marble) plus a WebGL2 splat renderer (Spark) make larger moves possible in the browser. They add cost and risk drifting from the painted style.

### Cited Findings
- Pillars: orthographic camera at "the perfect angle" is the only view where the illusion holds. — [Obsidian Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-)
- Deadfire added parallaxing background layers that scroll at different rates, with VFX blurred together with them. — [RPG Codex, Fig Update #11](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=10542)
- Banner Saga built every scene from pieces that move against each other, earning the joke name "The Parallax Saga". — [GameBanshee, Banner Saga art blog](https://gamebanshee.com/news/108934-the-banner-saga-art-blog-on-combat.html)
- Spine users deform background cliffs with FFD meshes for parallax "instead of just moving it like regular 2D parallaxing". This is a cheap way to get perspective-like parallax inside a single layer. — [Esoteric Software forum](http://de.esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/2602-using-spine-to-drive-environment-parallaxing)
- Pentiment: track-based movement for framing control, a fixed north orientation, and the manuscript's page turns done as boned 3D meshes. — [Game Developer deep dive](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/deep-dive-the-art-of-pentiment); [Sawyer interview](https://gamebanshee.com/9ak6r)
- Kentucky Route Zero's camera language is theatrical stagecraft and slow cinema: fixed, stage-like framings. — [GDC Vault abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1020596/Scenography-of-Kentucky-Route)
- Camera projection (film VFX): "camera projection can transform a 2D matte painting into a 3D scene (2.5D technique), recreating through the camera movement the depth effect". The workflow is to break the painting into layers, build rough geometry, project the painting from the shot camera, then move a second camera. — [3DArt.it, Nuke camera projection tutorial](https://www.3dart.it/tutorial/3d-tutorial/digital-compositing-software/nuke-tutorial/camera-projection-tutorial/); [The Rookies: camera mapping tips](https://discover.therookies.co/2020/03/22/matte-painting-tips-for-camera-mapping-3d-projection-and-element-integration-with-nuke/)
- 3D Ken Burns (Niklaus et al., 2019) predicts depth, refines it at object boundaries, lifts the image to a point cloud, and inpaints color and depth in disoccluded regions along the camera path. — [arXiv 1909.05483](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.05483)
- One Shot 3D Photography (Kopf et al., Facebook, 2020): mobile depth net, then a layered depth image (LDI), then inpainting of occluded color and geometry, then a mesh "rendered on low-end devices". It takes about 1.1 s on an iPhone 11 Pro. The companion paper describes the shipped Facebook 3D Photos feature. — [arXiv 2008.12298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12298); [Practical 3D Photography](https://johanneskopf.de/publications/photo3d_practical/Practical_3D_Photography.pdf)
- 3D Photography with context-aware layered depth inpainting (Shih et al., CVPR 2020): LDI with explicit pixel connectivity, inpainting of color and depth in occluded regions, renderable "with motion parallax on standard graphics engines". The code is MIT-licensed. — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Shih_3D_Photography_Using_Context-Aware_Layered_Depth_Inpainting_CVPR_2020_paper.html)
- SynSin (CVPR 2020): single image, then a predicted point cloud, then a differentiable render to the new view, then a GAN refiner that fills gaps. — [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/html/Wiles_SynSin_End-to-End_View_Synthesis_From_a_Single_Image_CVPR_2020_paper.html)
- Current (2025–26): World Labs Marble turns a text prompt, single image or panorama into a navigable 3D Gaussian-splat world. Exports are .spz/.ply splats plus collider and visual meshes. Limited beta was September 2025, formal launch followed, and tiers are 1.0 Draft / 1.1 / 1.1 Plus. — [Radiance Fields](https://radiancefields.com/world-labs-formally-launches-marble-a-generative-world-model); [Scenario help](https://help.scenario.com/articles/4774055514-marble-by-world-labs-the-essentials); [BD TechTalks](https://bdtechtalks.com/2025/09/19/world-labs-marble-generative-world-model/)
- Spark (World Labs, MIT): a three.js Gaussian-splat renderer on WebGL2 (">98% of devices"). Suggested budgets: Android phone 1–2M splats, iPhone 1–3M. Spark 2.0 (April 2026) added streamed LoD with a fixed GPU splat pool (default 16M). 2.4.0 reads SPZ v4. — [GitHub sparkjsdev/spark](https://github.com/sparkjsdev/spark); [Spark performance docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/performance/); [Spark 2.0 blog](https://worldlabs.ai/blog/spark-2.0); [Radiance Fields on 2.4.0](https://radiancefields.com/spark-2.4.0-reads-spz-v4-files-and-opens-paged-lod-to-custom-fetchers)
- Depth estimation, current: Depth Anything 3 (ByteDance Seed, November 2025) succeeds V2. DA3METRIC-LARGE has about 0.35B parameters, a DINOv2 ViT-L backbone, metric depth plus sky segmentation, and ONNX exports. Licence reports conflict (Apache 2.0 vs non-commercial for other family members), and so do metric conversion formulas. — [ComfyUI wiki](https://comfyui-wiki.com/en/models/depth-anything/depth-anything-3); [HF ONNX export](https://huggingface.co/Heliosoph/da3metric-large-onnx); [arXiv 2511.10647](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10647)
- The simple depth-parallax shader: offset UVs by depth × view direction × strength, driven by mouse or gyro. Dark pixels are far and light pixels near. — [Alan Zucconi: Parallax shaders & depth maps](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10453); [three.js 2.5D parallax example (base + depth + normal + noise maps)](https://freefrontend.com/code/halloween-2-5d-parallax-shader-2026-05-07/)

### Inferences
- Recommended camera vocabulary for the project, cheapest first:
  - (a) A slow orthographic pan and zoom ("Ken Burns") on the painting with no reprojection. Always safe.
  - (b) "Breathing": sub-pixel to a few pixels of depth-parallax sway, driven by time or gyro. Errors are invisible at small amplitudes.
  - (c) Zoom-to-inset, which the project already has. Cross-fade with a slight dolly-in and depth-of-field on the inset edges, a film iris/vignette match.
  - (d) A camera-projection move: project the painting from the canonical camera onto the calibrated blockout plus depth mesh and allow about ±5–10° orbit. Fill disocclusions with a pre-inpainted "back layer" generated by the image model, i.e. a manual LDI with two layers, near and far.
  - (e) For true "behind the building" views, generate a second painting from the second blockout camera and match-cut or cross-dissolve. Don't extrapolate one painting.
- Splat worlds (Marble + Spark) could drive a rare "establishing shot" fly-in. They are not a good base for every location on a phone: memory, style drift from the painted look, and the 1–2M splat budget on Android. They are optional for an Android build later.
- Transitions that fit a 19th-century book / engraving framing: Pentiment-style page turn (a boned mesh or a simple curl shader), iris in/out (a circular vignette mask, period-appropriate for magic-lantern and early cinema), and an ink-wash dissolve using the painting's luminance as a threshold map. All are a single fullscreen pass.

### Gaps
- I found no published camera-language talk specific to Disco Elysium, Pillars or Pentiment (zoom ranges, framing rules), and no shipped-game source for iris or match-cut transitions. These are left as design inferences.
- I didn't find benchmarks for LDI/mesh 3D-photo rendering inside three.js on mid-range Android.

## 3. VFX tricks (flow maps, UV-distortion water, caustics, depth fog, god rays, particles, cloud shadows, wind, shimmer, LUTs, grain/vignette, DOF/tilt-shift, bloom) and keeping them consistent with a painting

### Takeaway
Nearly every effect used by the reference games is a cheap fullscreen or decal-space shader driven by an authored mask and a scrolling noise or flow texture. INSIDE's slides are the strongest evidence of how far simple tricks go: linear fog, two glows, half-resolution blue-noise volumetrics, gradient-LUT fire, vertex-shader rain. The main rule for staying consistent with a painting is to drive color from the painting itself. Examples: sample the background for ambient, map emissive or fire luminance through a palette LUT, tint shadows to the painted shadow hue, and dither every 8-bit write.

### Cited Findings
- **Flow maps (Valve):** a low-resolution flow texture (about 4 texels per meter in Portal 2) holds 2D vectors that distort the normal map along the flow. Two layers offset by half a phase are cross-faded to hide the reset. Color/foam uses the same two-phase blend, and foam lookup is further distorted by the flowed normal to match ripple frequency. — [Vlachos, Water Flow in Portal 2, SIGGRAPH 2010](https://www.advances.realtimerendering.com/s2010/Vlachos-Waterflow(SIGGRAPH%202010%20Advanced%20RealTime%20Rendering%20Course).pdf); [Bronwen Grimes, GDC 2011](https://www.hlportal.de/files/GDC2011_BronwenGrimes_NonstandardTextures.pdf)
- **Painted water in practice (Disco Elysium):** a flat sea plane with edge fade, a recolorable warm overlay with a flow map for current, and distortion on transparent planes for refraction and ripples. — [From Render to Paintover](https://www.gamebanshee.com/news/121823-disco-elysium-from-render-to-paintover.html)
- **Fake caustics:** INSIDE uses an animated caustics cookie texture inside the volumetric light. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- **Fog:** INSIDE's main look is global linear fog clamped to a maximum so bright lights punch through. Local box volumes add only in-scattered light. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- **Volumetric fog with blue-noise ray offsets:** blue noise as a per-pixel ray start offset. — [demofox: ray marching fog with blue noise](https://blog.demofox.org/2020/05/10/ray-marching-fog-with-blue-noise/)
- **God rays in screen space:** the standard post-process radially blurs an occlusion-masked buffer toward the light's screen position (Mitchell, GPU Gems 3 ch. 13; used in Crysis-era games). It needs a large kernel, which can be done at downsampled resolution, and disappears when the light is behind the camera. — [GPU Gems 3, ch. 13](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-ii-light-and-shadows/chapter-13-volumetric-light-scattering-post-process); [Khronos forum, "Sunbeams in Crysis"](https://community.khronos.org/t/sunbeams-in-crysis/54219)
- **Cloud shadows and wind:** Deadfire projects drifting cloud shadows onto the level and drives trees, ocean, particles and capes from a shared wind. — [RPG Codex, Fig Update #11](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=10542)
- **Rain, snow and wetness on a painting:** a Disco Elysium top-down occlusion map keeps covered areas dry. Wetness is added to spec, and splashes go along detected edges and eaves. — [Weather Duty](https://discoelysium.com/devblog/2016/02/10/weather-duty)
- **Particles at near-zero CPU cost:** INSIDE's rain and dust are vertex-shader-only (position seeded from floor(time), animated by fract(time)). Smoke gets a vertical light gradient plus world-space swirl-noise distortion. Fire is luminance-only, mapped through a gradient LUT. Flipbooks use 3×3 frames, sequential column and random row. Lens flares are occluded by vertex-shader depth sampling. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- **Bloom and glow:** INSIDE uses separate wide (fog) and narrow glows, and "If bloom [is] calculated with a certain intensity, we need to show source pixel with the same intensity". Pillars has an optional subtle bloom on everything. Deadfire has "tight highlights bloom on metals". — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf); [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-); [RPG Codex](https://rpgcodex.net/article.php?id=10542)
- **Dithering and banding (critical for dark night scenes):** use TPDF or blue noise dither on every 8-bit write and after every pass. Plain uniform noise leaves visible "noise modulation" bands. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf); [Bart Wronski](https://bartwronski.com/2016/10/30/dithering-part-three-real-world-2d-quantization-dithering/); also see the three.js forum on bloom banding: [discourse](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/is-the-best-practice-a-dark-background-for-bloom-to-avoid-banding/72377)
- **Color grading, grain, vignette in three.js:** LUTPass (`three/addons/postprocessing/LUTPass.js`, 3D LUT texture) and FilmPass (grain intensity 0–1, grayscale flag) are built-in addons. INSIDE's glare pass "adds a bit of vignetting". Limbo relies on film grain and heavy DOF. — [three.js LUTPass docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/LUTPass.html); [three.js FilmPass docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/FilmPass.html); [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf); [Limbo slides](https://medienwissenschaft.uni-bayreuth.de/wp-content/uploads/assets/Lehre/Wintersemester-2012-13/klsg6/KLSG6-Limbo.pdf)
- **Stable stylised dithering (engraving or 1-bit look):** use blue-noise dither, which survives scaling and video compression. Anchor the pattern to the camera sphere or world, not to screen pixels, to stop it swimming. — [Zucconi on Obra Dinn](https://www.alanzucconi.com/?p=10365)
- **2D normal-mapped lighting in engines:** Unity URP 2D needs `_NormalMap` and `_MaskTex` secondary textures on the sprite's UVs (max 8). Normal maps add a full-size depth pre-pass per layer batch, a noted performance cost. — [Unity 6.2 docs: Secondary Textures](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.2/Documentation/Manual/urp/SecondaryTextures.html); [Unity 6.6 docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/SecondaryTextures.html)

### Inferences
- **Moonlit Kuindzhi night scenes:**
  - The candle point light should use half-Lambert wrap like INSIDE's bounce lights, since estimated normals are noisy, with falloff that clamps rather than goes physically correct.
  - Bloom should be two-tier: a wide, very-low-threshold "mist glow" and a narrow emissive glow on windows, candle and moon. Computed at half or quarter resolution.
  - Finish with TPDF or blue-noise dithering in the final pass to keep dark gradients smooth.
- **Fire, candles and window glow:** use INSIDE's gradient-LUT approach. Write intensity only, then map through a palette ramp sampled from the painting's own warm colors. Effects then cannot drift outside the painting's palette.
- **Mist:** keep the current ground mist, and apply depth-based linear fog using blockout depth (more stable than estimated depth). Clamp it so emissives punch through, as INSIDE does.
- **God rays:** the moon through trees and candle through windows can be a GPU Gems-style radial blur at quarter resolution, masked by the sky and window masks instead of a geometry occlusion pass.
- **Engraving style:** an optional blue-noise or line-hatch threshold pass anchored to world or painting UVs (Obra Dinn's lesson), not screen-space, so hatching doesn't swim during zoom.
- **Tilt-shift / miniature feel:** a depth-driven blur from the existing depth buffer is cheap at half resolution. Use it sparingly, because it fights the painting's own focus.

### Gaps
- No primary three.js source found for a built-in tilt-shift pass (three.js has BokehPass; tilt-shift needs a custom ShaderPass). Unverified.
- No sources found on "heat or moonlight shimmer" as a named technique in the reference games. It's a variant of UV distortion with a noise texture, by inference.
- No reference-game source on fireflies, bats or birds as specific particle setups. INSIDE's vertex-shader particle method applies by inference.

## 4. Making a static painting feel alive (ambient loops, mask-driven foliage, layered water, cinemagraphs)

### Takeaway
Shipped games don't animate the painting. They place separate animated elements on and around it: real-time plants on transparent planes, fish, gulls, ripples (Disco Elysium); animated overlay tiles for water (Infinity Engine); sliding painted pieces (Banner Saga); world-space distortion noise (INSIDE). Research has also shown fully automatic single-image water loops (Eulerian motion fields). For this project, the best results come from a few authored loops placed with masks plus 1–3 small critter loops per scene, rather than global wobble.

### Cited Findings
- Disco Elysium keeps NPCs, vegetation, collectables and particles out of the painting. It adds circling fish swarms, surface ripples, wobbling icebergs and flying seagulls as real-time life. Plants in water are transparent planes with distortion. — [From Render to Paintover](https://www.gamebanshee.com/news/121823-disco-elysium-from-render-to-paintover.html)
- The Infinity Engine shipped animated background tiles and overlays for rivers and lakes as separate tile grids. — [IESDP WED](https://gibberlings3.github.io/iesdp/file_formats/ie_formats/wed_v1.3.htm); [PBG features](https://pbg.bgforge.net/bg/info/features/index.html)
- Pillars: everything pre-rendered "apart from a few animated elements". — [Polycount mini-dump (snippet)](https://polycount.com/discussion/150819/pillars-of-eternity-mini-dump)
- Banner Saga: painted pieces move against each other "to give the illusion of movement". — [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/news/108934-the-banner-saga-art-blog-on-combat.html)
- INSIDE: world-space projected "swirl noise" scrolled in one direction animates smoke and foam. Random per-particle offsets prevent visible repetition. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- Eulerian motion fields (Holynski et al., CVPR 2021): a single static flow field predicted from one photo animates fluids (water, smoke) by warping deep features. Seamless loops come from running forward and backward in time and blending. Examples include rivers, waterfalls and beaches. — [Project page](https://eulerian.cs.washington.edu/); [CVPR paper](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2021/papers/Holynski_Animating_Pictures_With_Eulerian_Motion_Fields_CVPR_2021_paper.pdf)
- Spine FFD deformation of background meshes is an alternative to rigid layer motion. — [Esoteric forum](http://de.esotericsoftware.com/forum/d/2602-using-spine-to-drive-environment-parallaxing)

### Inferences
- The Eulerian idea maps directly onto the project's existing water mask. Author or derive one static flow texture per location and animate with Valve's two-phase flow-map blend. That is the real-time equivalent of the paper's forward/backward loop blend, and costs 2 texture reads.
- A practical "life budget" per location is 2–4 independent, non-synchronised motions at different periods (e.g. water 6–10 s loop, foliage 2–4 s sway, one window flicker, one critter event every 15–40 s), each confined by a mask. This is an inference from the Disco Elysium practice of a few targeted live elements.
- Critter loops (bats, owls, a dog, a smoking chimney, laundry) can be short sprite flipbooks generated by an image or video model and placed on the blockout with depth so they occlude correctly. INSIDE's "sequential column, random row" flipbook trick reduces visible repetition with few frames.
- Keep animated elements as separate transparent planes or quads drawn over the painting, as Disco Elysium does, instead of warping the painting itself where possible. Warping the painting smears the brushwork. Overlays keep it intact.

### Gaps
- I didn't find benchmarks or shipped-game uses of modern image-to-video models (2025–26) for seamless cinemagraph loops in games. Their use in this pipeline is unverified.
- No shipped-game source on character reflections in painted water.

## 5. Performance on mid-range phones (texture sizes, KTX2/Basis, number of passes, fill-rate, resolution scaling)

### Takeaway
For a phone-browser painted-background game, the binding constraints are GPU memory (uncompressed big textures) and fill-rate or bandwidth (fullscreen passes on tile-based GPUs), not geometry. Keep individual textures at or below 4096 px, since only about 59% of Android WebGL2 devices report 8192. Ship colors as KTX2 (ETC1S for paintings, UASTC for normals and data), downsample smooth data (depth, fog, bloom, volumetrics) to half or quarter resolution, merge effects into as few fullscreen passes as possible, and cap devicePixelRatio per device tier.

### Cited Findings
- Android WebGL2 `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE`: 4096 is reported on 99.99% of Android devices and 8192 on about 59.43%, versus 94.52% overall. The collection date isn't stated. — [web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/parameters/MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE)
- Memory: "One 4096x4096px image with mipmaps consumes 90 MB of memory" when uploaded from PNG, JPEG, WebP or AVIF, because those are decoded to RGBA. GPU formats (ETC, ASTC, BC) give "memory cost… reduced by 4–8x, and uploads complete 4–8x faster." ETC1S is the "Default choice in most cases" (JPEG-like file size) but weaker on data textures. UASTC has "Quality… sufficient for… normal maps", files about 1–2× JPEG with Zstd, and pixel data "still 4x smaller than JPEG or PNG". Basis is transcoded at runtime to the device's GPU format. There is no universal HDR GPU format. Use `renderer.initTexture()` to pre-upload and `ImageBitmapLoader` to decode off the main thread. — [Don McCurdy, Choosing texture formats for WebGL/WebGPU (2024)](https://www.donmccurdy.com/2024/02/11/web-texture-formats/)
- KTX2/Basis combines ETC1- and ASTC-derived modes with RDO and LZ supercompression. ETC1S gives much smaller transmission and memory sizes than JPEG or PNG, and UASTC suits normal maps. — [Khronos KTX 2.0 announcement via GfxSpeak](https://gfxspeak.com/featured/khronos-compresses-everyone/)
- Tile-based mobile GPUs: keeping data on-chip between dependent passes saved 45% read and 56% write bandwidth versus two separate passes (Vulkan subpasses on Mali). Load/store choices matter, since clearing at render-pass start is the efficient path. A fullscreen pass that reads the previous pass as a texture forces a round-trip to memory. — [Arm: Vulkan subpasses](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/vulkan-subpasses-the-good-the-bad-and-the-ugly); [Arm: load/store ops](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/picking-the-most-efficient-load-store-operations); [Arm: Mali tile-based rendering](https://developer.arm.com/community/arm-community-blogs/b/mobile-graphics-and-gaming-blog/posts/the-mali-gpu-an-abstract-machine-part-2---tile-based-rendering)
- INSIDE's approach targeted console and PC, but its lessons transfer. The volumetrics are "primarily bandwidth bound", so fewer samples (3 with blue noise vs 32), half-resolution buffers, and letting TAA integrate the undersampled effect. Shadow maps for static lights update over several frames. — [INSIDE slides](https://loopit.dk/rendering_inside.pdf)
- three.js on weak phones: developers report fixed pixel ratios of about 0.5–0.8 on weak phones. Continuously adapting the ratio to FPS causes crisp/blurry swings, so the advice is to measure at startup and snap to a tier. Post-processing passes may not inherit the renderer's pixel ratio, so size every pass explicitly. — [three.js forum: weak devices](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/searching-ways-for-flexible-performance-improvement-on-weak-devices/2439); [three.js forum: pixelRatio based on FPS](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/changing-pixelratio-based-on-fps-good-or-bad-idea/34563); [three.js forum: postprocessing pixelation](https://discourse.threejs.org/t/postprocessing-adds-a-lot-of-pixelation/55813?page=2)
- Unity 2D lights with normal maps add a full-size depth pre-pass per layer batch, a known cost of normal-mapped 2D lighting. — [Unity 6.6 docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.6/Documentation/Manual/urp/SecondaryTextures.html)
- Asset scale in shipped games: Pillars' raw background data reached "several gigabytes" before compression, and backgrounds were about 10k×10k px. Disco Elysium had about 193 4K texture squares ×4 maps. — [Update #79](https://eternity.obsidian.net/eternity/news/update--79-graphics-and-rendering-); [GameBanshee](http://www.gamebanshee.com/kdh63); [MCV](https://www.mcvuk.com/business-news/we-knew-immediately-that-we-needed-to-make-a-game-with-a-striking-and-unique-look-to-accompany-the-writing-a-look-that-would-balance-the-mundane-with-the-unfamiliar-and-strange-the-art)
- Splat budget, if used: Android phone 1–2M splats (Spark guidance). — [Spark performance docs](https://sparkjs.dev/docs/performance/)

### Inferences
- **Budget sketch for one location.** Arithmetic from McCurdy's formula (w × h × 4 × 1.333):
  - Painting: a 4096×4096 RGBA8 painting with mips is about 90 MB uncompressed. As ETC1S or UASTC transcoded to ETC2 or ASTC 4×4 (8 bpp), it's about 22 MB.
  - Data maps (depth, normal, masks): put them at half resolution (2048²) and pack them. For example normal.xy + depth in one RGB UASTC texture, and water/foliage/window/sky masks in one RGBA ETC1S or UASTC texture. That's about 5–6 MB each compressed.
  - The detail insets load on demand.
  - Resulting total: about 35–45 MB GPU per location, versus about 270+ MB if everything were uncompressed full-resolution PNG.
- **Very large paintings** (e.g. 8k for pan-and-zoom): tile into 2048² or 4096² KTX2 tiles, Infinity Engine style, to stay under the 4096 limit and allow streaming. Compute the zoom-inset resolution from the zoom factor rather than storing the whole painting at insane resolution (PoE's 10k×10k is a desktop figure).
- **Pass structure.** Aim for one main pass: painting + relief + water/foliage/flicker + candle light + fog, all in one shader reading the packed maps. Then a character pass with the depth test against blockout depth. Then at most one half-resolution bloom chain. Then one final composite pass doing LUT + vignette + grain + dither + iris. That is 3–4 passes instead of 8–10 EffectComposer passes. Merging passes matters most on tile-based Mali and Adreno GPUs.
- **Resolution tiers.** Pick a tier at startup (DPR cap of 1.0 / 1.5 / 2.0, or render-scale 0.66–1.0) and keep it fixed. Run blur-type effects at half or quarter resolution. Painted backgrounds hide upscaling well, but characters and text don't, so render UI at native resolution.

### Gaps
- No found benchmarks of fullscreen-pass cost (ms per pass at 1080p) on specific 2024–26 mid-range Android GPUs (e.g. Mali-G57/G68, Adreno 6xx/7xx) in WebGL2.
- The web3dsurvey collection period isn't stated, so the 59% 8192 figure may be outdated.
- No verified data on KTX2 transcode time on mid-range phones for 4096² textures.
